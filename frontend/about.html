About Themis

The problem

Banks and payment apps mostly catch unauthorized fraud — a stolen password, a hacked account. They largely miss authorized-but-deceived fraud, where the real account holder is coerced into paying willingly. RBI (Reserve Bank of India, the country's central bank) now classifies these as fraud too, but the detection gap remains.

What Themis sees

Themis adds signals a bank's systems don't have: whether a call was active during payment, whether a screen was being shared, whether an OTP was read aloud. These live at the app layer, not the bank's switch.

How it decides

A LightGBM model (a machine-learning model built from many small decision trees), calibrated with Platt scaling (a method that makes its 0-100% risk scores better match real-world probabilities), scores each transaction. The threshold is chosen by minimizing real cost — weighing the friction of a false alarm against the loss of a missed scam — not by chasing the highest accuracy number.

What it's allowed to do

A single Python invariant (a rule enforced directly in code) enforces this: Themis can only ever issue a bounded cooling-off delay or an advisory notice. It cannot block a payment, reverse funds, or touch another account — that boundary is checked in code on every decision, not just documented.
